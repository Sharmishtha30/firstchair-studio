- Lower per-drop rate: Work out the saving at the delivery volume you expect.

- Three months exclusive: Check which backup options the commitment would rule out.

- Penalty for using others: Weigh the saving against the cost of turning to another partner.

Layout: checklist; image: none

Caption:

In Proving Ground’s Diwali Surge practice scenario, the delivery partner who walked out returns with a lower per-drop rate, three months of exclusivity and a penalty if you use anyone else.

Before recommending the deal, separate the saving from the restriction. What would the lower rate save at your expected volume? Which backup options would exclusivity rule out? What would using another partner cost?

Your recommendation needs to explain why the saving is—or isn’t—worth giving up that flexibility.

Both matter. They answer different questions.

- Decision evidence: The alternatives, the trade-off and your reason for choosing.

Slide 4: **Build one defensible interview example**

Start with a decision you genuinely owned.

- Reconstruct the options: Name the realistic alternatives available at the time.

- Expose the trade-off: Explain the constraint and what your choice sacrificed.

- Defend the reasoning: Use what you knew then; separate your rationale from the outcome.

Layout: process; image: none

Slide 5: **Test the work before the application**

Try Proving Ground’s three-call decision exercise to see what the standard asks of your reasoning before deciding whether to apply.

Caption:

A delivery summary can establish that work got done without explaining how you think.

When preparing an interview example, separate the outcome from the decision. Identify the alternatives that were genuinely available, the constraint that mattered and the trade-off you accepted. Then explain what you knew at the time—not just what became obvious afterwards.

Be precise about ownership. If you recommended an option but someone else approved it, say so. If the outcome is unknown, leave it unknown.
