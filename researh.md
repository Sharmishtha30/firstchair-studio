# Copywriting research

Version 0.5 • 5 October 2026. Filename retained as requested. This is the research record; [recommendation.md](recommendation.md) contains our working product decisions.

## Evidence levels

Research findings, practitioner advice and our creative hypotheses are different. A result from website usability or newspaper sharing does not directly establish Instagram carousel conversion. Frameworks are tools, not proven universal sequences. Proposed examples are not performance winners.

## Reading effort

Nielsen Norman Group's website-writing studies favour concise, scannable and objective writing. Application hypothesis: clear hierarchy and meaningful chunks help social readers understand the post. Do not infer a carousel lift from website usability measurements or impose a fixed word count on all categories.

Source: [Applying Writing Guidelines to Web Pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-writing-guidelines-web-pages/).

## Curiosity

Loewenstein's account relates curiosity to a perceived gap in understanding. Application hypothesis: an opening should make a relevant question understandable and the post should answer it. Vague withheld secrets are a poor default. Curiosity alone does not establish relevance or buying intent.

Source: [The Psychology of Curiosity](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf), 1994.

## Sharing

Berger and Milkman's newspaper analysis and experiments connect sharing with practical usefulness, surprise and emotional activation. Their work distinguishes emotional activation from simple positive/negative tone. Application hypothesis: useful distinctions, recognition and vivid experiences can be productive angles. Do not interpret this as permission to manufacture fear or a prediction of purchases.

Source: [What Makes Online Content Viral?](https://jonahberger.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/ViralityB.pdf).

## Customer language

Message research uses interviews and surveys to identify language and buying context. A public website chiefly describes the company's intended positioning. If customer evidence is absent, motivations remain hypotheses. Avoid attributing invented thoughts or dialogue to customers.

Source: [Mining Messages From Your Customers](https://lite.cxl.com/m/Ix0edDbE/sales-copywriting-and-product-messaging-mining-messages-from-your-customers).

## Framework library

Definitions below are concise practitioner summaries; use-case choices and cautions are our applications.

- PAS: problem, agitation, solution. Use for a recognised difficulty; bound the consequence rather than inflating fear.
- AIDA: attention, interest, desire, action. A general persuasion sequence, not compulsory slide labels.
- Before–After–Bridge: current situation, desired situation, route between them. Desired future is not proof of an achieved outcome.
- Feature–Advantage–Benefit: capability, practical difference, reader value. Make the benefit concrete and supported.
- Picture–Promise–Proof–Proposal: imaginable situation, bounded promise, substantiation, next step. Missing proof restricts the promise.
- Awareness: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware and ready to act. Match the opening to what the reader is likely to know; treat this as a hypothesis when evidence is thin.

Sources: [Copyhackers formula reference](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/), [Conversion Copywriting 101](https://school.copyhackers.com/courses/conversion-copywriting-101).

Our additional working structures: objection → answer → demonstration; familiar belief → overlooked distinction → example → action; genuine event → turning point → meaning. These are synthesis for this product, not separately validated psychological laws.

## Research questions to resolve

1. When does sensory desire outperform practical education in our readers' judgement?
2. Which mechanisms make professional-tool copy understandable without making it tedious?
3. When does a comparison clarify a real buying tradeoff rather than manufacture a straw-man alternative?
4. How much brand context does a static need to stand alone?
5. What makes slide two deliver enough value that the reader continues?
6. How do audience knowledge and product familiarity alter the best opening?
7. Can we improve copy consistently across new websites without customer evidence?

## Reference study protocol

Study full posts and captions. Label content purpose, proof and audience assumptions. Collect contrasts: clear/unclear, complete/incomplete payoff, genuine/unsupported evidence, sensory/instructional, product-first/situation-first. Preserve exceptions rather than inventing universal rules.

Visible engagement is not conversion evidence. Organic posts and paid acquisition ads have different contexts; keep that distinction. A chat/review-shaped creative may be persuasive-looking without being authentic proof.

## Early-stage companies — research update, 5 October 2026

### What changed and what remains unproven

Sam chose companies with little or no Instagram/LinkedIn presence as our customer focus. This gives a design constraint: the system cannot depend on a posting history, existing community or testimonials. It does not establish demand, channel suitability or willingness to pay. Product requirements below are our applications of the research, not published findings about Content Studio.

### Clear category before clever positioning

Michael Seibel's YC guidance recommends a comprehensible description of the company and a concrete user path, alongside a specific insight. Context: startup pitches, not controlled social-content research. Our application: a stranger should be able to locate the offer in an understandable category; a distinctive hook must not make the product obscure. Test category-first versus situation-first openings while holding facts constant.

Source: [How to Pitch Your Company](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company/), 2016.

### Expertise can be demonstrated through useful thinking

LinkedIn's account of the 2024 Edelman–LinkedIn survey describes decision-makers' reported trust and receptiveness toward strong thought leadership. This is B2B self-report evidence, not a causal test of carousel purchases. It suggests testing useful explanations and concrete decisions as credibility for unfamiliar business brands. It does not justify putting a generic inspirational quote over a product image or pretending that expertise guarantees demand.

Source: [Why B2B Thought Leadership Matters and How to Get It Right](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/content-marketing/the-importance-of-b2b-thought-leadership-content-and-how-to-get-it-right), 2025.

### Transfer limits of established-brand references

The Ordinary, Oatly and other established brands can rely on familiarity, distribution and prior context. Early-stage brands may lack those advantages. Tag each reference's assumed prior knowledge, required assets and required proof. Retain the communication mechanism, then test whether it works when the logo is unknown. Paid supplement offers should not become universal organic startup templates.

### Questions for Sam's manual research

For selected examples, note: could an unfamiliar reader identify the product? What value arrives before the sales request? What claim depends on proof? Does the image demonstrate the idea or merely decorate it? Is the CTA possible for a company without an established audience? No need to answer every question for every post; prioritise the reason you chose it.

Include a few early-stage brands alongside established references when available. Do not assume few followers mean the company is young or unsuccessful. Record observable account context separately from guesses.

### Proposed comparison study

Use a small set of readable sites spanning a business tool, a consumer product and a service. Prefer examples with no supplied testimonials as well as examples with genuine proof. Keep source evidence and visual treatment comparable. Compare old and proposed copy without telling reviewers which is newer. Ask what the company offers, who it is relevant to, what the post taught them and what they would do next. Record confusion and mistaken claims, not only preference.

Success before implementation: stronger understanding and a specific worthwhile payoff across categories, with no unsupported claims. This is an editorial checkpoint; growth outcomes need actual publication, distribution context and later analytics.

### Customer-demand validation, separate from creative quality

In founder conversations, ask about the last post they tried to produce, where it stalled, who did the work, how long it took and what they actually published. Ask which generated creative they would use and what they would change. Observe a real website-to-download attempt. Avoid treating enthusiasm for a demo as evidence of repeat use or payment. No interviews have been completed for this update.

## Website asset extraction update
Collect a reusable public-site asset library before writing: homepage plus up to four linked product, about or review pages; retain up to 24 raster images, icons and sanitised SVG illustrations, with original asset and page URLs. Keep CSS font declarations and matched element typography (family, size, weight, line height, letter spacing, casing, colour and radius) as labelled visual observations. Import source heading font files using available normal weights and correct file formats. Website pixel sizes describe web hierarchy; social text must retain readable minimum sizes. Preserve exact website testimonials and visible attribution as source statements, never independent verification or permission to invent chat screenshots, ratings or outcomes. Visual planning receives at most six supported raster inputs; the larger library remains available by asset key. Missing dynamic pages, protected content, unavailable fonts and omitted CSS effects must be disclosed. This is bounded extraction, not a guarantee of every asset on every page or computed browser styling.


## Copy perspective: who speaks, to whom, and how it lands

Research update: 5 October 2026. These are design hypotheses for our generator, not evidence of conversion lift. Brand voice, viewpoint, audience, tone and framework are separate decisions.

- **Voice:** the brand’s stable character and vocabulary. Mailchimp distinguishes stable voice from tone that changes with the reader’s emotional situation. Our application: keep source character across packs while changing the argument. [Mailchimp voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/).
- **Tone:** intensity and manner in a particular post. Nielsen Norman Group describes humor, formality, respectfulness and enthusiasm as dimensions. Their website findings do not establish Instagram performance; avoid equating “strong” with loud, disrespectful or alarmist. [NN/G tone dimensions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/).
- **Reader and buying context:** the actual buyer, what they are considering, and what makes the offer relevant. April Dunford connects positioning to best-fit customers, differentiated value, alternatives and market context. Our application: a broad market label alone cannot produce a good post. [Positioning guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning).
- **Customer language:** real interviews/reviews can reveal what a buyer cares about; choosing a customer viewpoint does not authorize invented first-person outcomes. Copyhackers demonstrates using audience insight to help people recognize themselves in a brand. [Voice of customer in a brand](https://copyhackers.com/voice-of-customer-in-your-brand/).

### Observed examples and why they speak differently

1. Basecamp’s current homepage says “project management without all the nonsense.” It names the category and takes a clear product position. The reader is a team choosing a work tool; the claim should be read as brand positioning, not independently proven superiority. [Basecamp](https://basecamp.com/).
2. Patagonia says “We're in business to save our home planet.” The speaker is the organization, and the content expresses a belief rather than explaining a feature. A young brand needs evidence of its own choices before adopting this approach; copying another brand’s cause would be false. [Patagonia mission](https://www.patagonia.com/footer-quote.html).
3. Copyhackers’ historical GitPrime example, “Engineers build business,” reframes the people affected by the product as valuable contributors. The article distinguishes the engineer from the purchasing decision-maker. Our application: buyer and end user can be different; naming the wrong reader can undermine otherwise polished copy. This is a historical practitioner example, not our performance result. [Copyhackers case discussion](https://copyhackers.com/voice-of-customer-in-your-brand/).
4. Maid2Clean’s FAQs specify a telephone interview, a home interview, identity/address and reference checks, ongoing monitoring and a key acknowledgement form. These details supply proof for a trust or convenience story. They do not justify claims of zero risk or satisfaction. [Maid2Clean FAQs](https://www.maid2clean.co.uk/faqs/).

### Five viewpoints available in the product

The following lines are **illustrative rewrites by us**, not live ads, testimonials or tested winners. Same Maid2Clean evidence, different entry points:

| Viewpoint | Who speaks / who reads | Example opening | Intended response | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Product voice | Brand to a buyer comparing services | Before a cleaner joins us, the checks begin. | Understand the mechanism behind “vetted.” | Actual screening steps. |
| Customer perspective | Brand addresses the household decision-maker | Who’s coming into your home? Here’s what we check first. | Recognize the hesitation and see a concrete answer. | Screening process; no invented customer quote. |
| Market perspective | Brand addresses people considering the category | Choosing a cleaner also means choosing who enters your home. | Reframe a category decision. | A bounded, supported situation; no claim about everyone. |
| Helpful guide | Brand helps the buyer make one decision | Your cleaner’s first interview happens before yours. | Discover a useful detail, then understand the process. | Source distinction between agency screening and client meeting. |
| Brand belief | Brand states a documented operating choice | Acceptance is where ongoing monitoring starts. | See how the service follows through. | Documented ongoing monitoring; no fabricated values manifesto. |

“Voice of the market” is not an established universal copywriting formula. Our label denotes a category viewpoint. A website can show how a brand describes a category, but it cannot establish market consensus, consumer prevalence or a trend. Broader trend content needs additional named, current sources. V1 therefore narrows to a documented situation when wider evidence is absent.

“User of the user” could also mean the buyer’s own customer: for example a scheduling product addressing a salon owner by explaining the client’s booking experience. That is a distinct reader relationship, not a synonym for customer voice. Pending clarification, the implemented choice means the brand’s buyer. Do not silently substitute an end-customer persona.

### How copy lands: a practical decision record

For each candidate, record: speaker; one addressee; their observable moment; awareness hypothesis; content job; desired reaction; opening promise; supported detail that delivers it; visual proof; earned action. Awareness/emotion are hypotheses unless the source establishes them.

Example content jobs: desire/discovery, recognition, objection resolution, explanation, identity/belief, practical decision. A framework arranges the chosen job; it cannot replace choosing a strong idea. PAS fits a recognized difficulty, sensory demonstration fits desire, objection–answer–proof fits hesitation, and belief–choice–demonstration fits a supported point of view. These are recommendations to test, not compulsory slide templates.

Reject a draft if its hook could belong to almost any business, if it promises a payoff the slides do not deliver, if it speaks to an invented audience, or if its credibility depends on fake customer/market proof. Keep a genuine source condition but remove disclaimers about promises the post never made. Strong means clear and earned, not exaggerated.

### Learning from use

A download is a product event, not proof that the post resonated or converted. One edit provides qualitative preference data, not a ranking signal by itself. Later learning needs explicit acceptance/rejection, the exact edit reason, published results and comparable exposure before choosing winners. No automatic internet scraping or self-training is implemented by this change.


## Observations from fresh-category tests (5 October 2026)

These are qualitative observations from our local generator, not controlled experiments or performance research. Source websites were read in the browser and the PNG downloads inspected. Before/after examples also vary because generation is nondeterministic; do not attribute every improvement to a particular wording change.

**Software — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/).** A specific free-plan distinction was more concrete than a broad efficiency promise: the source pricing allowance is a refillable queue, not a monthly posting quota. The carousel demonstrated a channel adaptation example. It remained more explanatory than distinctive brand storytelling. A future test should compare that practical demonstration against a documented brand belief or product-led opening, while retaining exact plan conditions.

**Physical work tools — [Ugmonk](https://ugmonk.com/).** Today, Next and Someday cards supplied a recognizable tension: writing down a future idea does not make it today's commitment. Initial export ended in a kit contents list; the fresh rerun resolved the tension and invited exploring Analog kits. The distinct static kept a working sketch visible beside a task list. Both use sourced product mechanisms, with the designer's workday explicitly an example. Browser styles confirmed Albra Book Regular for the hero and GT Pressura Standard for supporting text. Separate body fonts were previously collected as a name but not carried into exports; that implementation gap is corrected. A photo had also been classified as decoration and repeated in the footer; automatic raster decoration is now excluded.

**Footwear — [Allbirds](https://www.allbirds.com/).** The hero uses Geograph, while secondary sections use Self Modern. Selecting a generic serif token gave the wrong dominant typeface; extraction now follows a matching headline rule or the inherited body font before using an unrelated serif token. Initial related-page selection spent all four slots on catalogues that exceeded the existing two-megabyte reading limit (the inspected shop-all catalogue was about nine megabytes, while an inspected product page was under one megabyte). Product-specific links now rank ahead of collections. This is a website-ingestion issue that directly limits the specificity of copy, rather than evidence that the model cannot write about footwear.

Source coverage remains bounded. Dynamic imagery, protected reviews, variations by region and CSS specificity may differ from the stored public HTML. These examples establish observed fixes and creative hypotheses; they do not establish general readiness across every website, proven click-through or sales lift.


### Final export checks from the fresh-brand batch

Uniform empty margins in source product images can make the product unreadably small. The renderer now conservatively frames uniform or transparent padding while retaining all non-background pixels, shadows and aspect ratio. Uneven lifestyle backgrounds stay unchanged. An inline home-link header SVG can be the actual brand wordmark; collect and sanitize it when no linked logo was found. Allbirds live export verified both corrections across nine 1080×1350 images. Ugmonk and Buffer downloads were also visually reviewed. These are qualitative checks on three brands, not evidence of conversion lift or universal readiness. Automated reviewer approval still does not replace rendered image inspection.
