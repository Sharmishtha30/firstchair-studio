# Content Studio recommendations

Version 0.6 • 6 October 2026 • Working specification; core editorial policy implemented in the shared generator. Live quality checks are recorded separately.

Research basis: [researh.md](researh.md). Reference collection: [design/inspiration/GOLDEN_SET.md](design/inspiration/GOLDEN_SET.md).

## Product purpose

Any supplied public website should lead to useful, brand-specific Instagram and LinkedIn carousels and statics, ready to download and post manually. Start with a reader's situation and a worthwhile idea. Do not turn the website into a slide-by-slide summary. Preserve the website's business identity even when a campaign chooses one product. No reels, new paid provider or model training in this phase.

## Who we serve — confirmed product direction

Our ideal customer is an early-stage company with little or no Instagram and LinkedIn presence that needs brand-specific carousels and statics. This is Sam's chosen product focus, not a validated market-demand finding. Likely operator: a founder or small marketing team; willingness to pay, frequency and time saved still need interviews and use evidence.

Keep two audiences separate: the company buying Content Studio and the person buying that company's product. Never make every generated post sound like it is for founders. A young skincare company still needs posts for skincare buyers.

Initial fit: an identifiable offer, a usable public website and a reachable buying audience. Lack of social history must not prevent generation. Weak website evidence limits what we can responsibly write; it does not justify a generic motivational post. Do not infer company stage, customer count or social absence from the website alone.

### What a first useful pack must achieve

A stranger should understand who the offer is for, why it matters, something specific about how it works and a sensible next step. These jobs can be spread across the pack; every post does not need the whole company pitch. Each creative must still make sense when encountered alone.

For the existing carousel-plus-static output, choose complementary jobs. A carousel can teach a useful buying distinction or demonstrate a process. The static can communicate a concrete product benefit, occasion or answered hesitation. Reject pairs that repeat the same slogan. This is a starting policy to test, not a fixed content ratio.

### Build credibility without invented social proof

Use what the company can actually show: a product screenshot, ingredients or materials, a documented process, a useful worked example, transparent terms or an explained design choice. These substantiate particular facts; they do not prove customer satisfaction or business outcomes.

If reviews, customer messages, results or credentials are absent, exclude testimonial and authority formats. Do not create fake chats, publication logos, follower counts, before/after outcomes or founder anecdotes. Founder voice is possible only from supplied statements; an invented first-person origin story is not acceptable. Format inspiration is separate from permission to assert its claims.

### Handling incomplete websites

Find the offer, audience and distinct mechanism before drafting. If the audience is inferred, label that in the internal brief and avoid confidently naming a narrow demographic. If one essential fact remains ambiguous, ask one concrete question, such as “Is this for individual job seekers or hiring teams?” Missing optional evidence should select another angle rather than block the pack.

Preserve the URL-to-creatives flow. Do not make users fill a long strategy questionnaire. Keep internal evidence and reasoning outside the public creative; show only a short correction request when it materially affects the output.

### Platform decisions

Create Instagram and LinkedIn variants from the same supported idea, with differences justified by the intended reader and use. For a work-related buyer, LinkedIn can develop the decision, tradeoff and example; for a consumer occasion, Instagram can foreground the product in use and sensory detail. These are defaults to test, not platform laws. Do not force a professional angle onto every consumer product or equate a LinkedIn user with a founder.

Check the next action against the actual publishing account and destination. A caption URL, profile link and comment request are different actions. Do not promise a profile link exists, an automated DM, a download or a demo unless the relevant path is verified. Where unknown, mark the publishing instruction for user confirmation rather than claim fulfilment.

## Writing decisions we can implement and inspect

For every draft, retain a short internal record:

- Company facts and source excerpts; inferred buyer and uncertainty.
- Reader situation and content job.
- Three distinct angle candidates and why one was selected.
- Hook and promised payoff; evidence supporting both.
- Slide purposes or the static's complete point; asset that adds meaning.
- Objection answered, genuine next action and missing publication details.
- Rejected claims and final editorial changes.

An unfamiliar brand must pass the stranger test: can a reader identify the product category and relevance from the post without knowing the brand name? The payoff test asks what the reader learns, sees or can decide after reading. “This is better” or “try our product” is insufficient. The swap test asks whether another company's name could replace ours unchanged; if so, add a genuine product detail or rethink the idea. These are editorial tests, not quantitative predictors of conversion.

Example using hypothetical, explicitly supplied scheduling-tool facts: booking links, availability rules and buffer settings. Weak hook: “Unlock effortless scheduling.” Better educational angle: “Your booking link is open. Does your calendar leave time between calls?” Payoff: show where buffers fit in an example schedule and how to set them. Do not claim it eliminates burnout or saves a number of hours. The stronger angle is still a hypothesis until compared with users.

## What we observed

Founder’s Find, Maid2Clean and Death Wish Coffee completed local generation/export checks. This proves the flow works for those runs, not that the copy performs well. Current copy is usually understandable but often chooses safe explanations over compelling buying situations. Source font matching and visual variety remain separate issues. Automated checks cannot establish marketing quality.

## 1. Build an evidence brief

Record the overall business, selected product, audience, supplied geography, mechanism, usable assets, genuine proof, actual offer terms and inspected CTA destination. Each claim needs a source excerpt and URL.

Separate observed facts, evidence-supported interpretation, audience hypotheses and unknowns. Website copy is the company's description, not automatically customer language. A fictional scenario is not a testimonial. Do not convert a wish into an outcome, drop a negation or generalise an individual result.

## 2. Choose the reader and content job

Define one specific reader moment: what happened, what they want, what is difficult, what alternatives they consider and what decision is next. Awareness—what the reader already knows about the problem and product—is a hypothesis unless evidence supports it.

Choose the primary job: recognition, practical education, desire, purchase evaluation, trust or action on an actual offer. Do not force pain into every category. Taste, identity, play, ritual and discovery can be stronger for some products. A useful post need not sell immediately, but its relationship to the brand must be clear.

## 3. Develop competing ideas before sentences

Draft at least three genuinely different angles, not three paraphrased hooks. For each record reader moment, opening, promised payoff, supporting evidence, brand relevance, framework, image relationship and next action.

Choose using recognisability, worthwhile discovery, specificity, credibility and brand fit. Reject an angle whose payoff is only an obvious instruction. A checklist is valid when the choices inside it teach something; it is not the default answer.

## 4. Select a structure that serves the idea

- Recognised difficulty: PAS, with proportionate consequences.
- Desire and exploration: AIDA, sensory demonstration or a credible before/after contrast.
- Product explanation: feature → practical difference → reader value.
- Purchase hesitation: objection → answer → demonstration.
- Educational distinction: familiar belief → overlooked distinction → example → action.
- Genuine story: event → turning point → meaning. Use a clearly framed hypothetical when no real event is available.

Frameworks organise the argument; they do not supply evidence or force particular slide headings. Proof-dependent formats are unavailable when proof is missing. Use genuine reviews, messages or study results only with accurate context and appropriate permission for publication.

## 5. Draft the whole post

Write opening and payoff together. A carousel should change understanding on every slide, without withholding useful information just to create a swipe. Current five-slide constraint is a product choice, not a universal rule. A static must communicate one complete idea without relying on its caption to reverse a misleading headline.

Introduce the product when it earns its role. Translate named features into concrete reader value, without inventing quantified benefits. Captions add useful context, limits and a truthful destination. One coherent next action per post; do not invent DM fulfilment or scarcity.

## 6. Edit in this order

1. Factual integrity and correct brand.
2. Reader relevance and strength of the idea.
3. Complete argument and delivered payoff.
4. Natural language, specific nouns/verbs, rhythm and brand voice.
5. Copy-image relationship and readable layout.

Do not polish an uninteresting idea indefinitely. Avoid blanket stylistic bans: questions, short sentences or familiar phrases can work when they serve meaning. Never shrink or truncate copy to conceal a layout failure.

## Writing workshop: three brands

These are proposed directions based on previously inspected website evidence, not customer quotes, deployed changes or tested winners. Recheck live claims before publication.

### Founder’s Find

A / Evidence gap: “How do you show judgement for a job you haven’t had?” Payoff: show how practice decisions, reasoning and assessment standards make capability inspectable. Framework: question → distinction → mechanism → demonstration → demo. Preferred carousel candidate because it connects a concrete transition problem to a supported mechanism.

B / Purchase evaluation: “Before you join a course, ask to see the feedback.” Payoff: inspect the standard, individual marks and actual answers. Preferred static candidate because it is complete and distinct from A.

C / Opportunity overload: “Finding a role is one decision. How you approach it is another.” Explore the supported job-board routes. Reserve until we can make the distinction more useful than an obvious statement.

### Death Wish Coffee

A / Taste discovery: “White chocolate. Toasted pistachio. Your next coffee?” Pair the actual product photo with supported flavour language. Preferred static candidate: vivid, specific and recognisably about a product.

B / Format choice: “Ground, beans or pods: start with the brewer you have.” Demonstrate concrete format differences, then connect to real listings. Useful but risks being mundane; keep as a practical carousel baseline.

C / Convenience: “Want a latte without brewing it?” Explain the actual canned-latte format. Potential carousel only if there is enough source-supported substance; do not pad one simple fact across five slides or invent energy/health claims.

### Maid2Clean

A / Control and reassurance: “Can you meet your cleaner before the first clean?” Answer the concern through the genuine meeting and approval process. Preferred carousel candidate; stronger reader stakes than generic cleaning benefits.

B / Purchase planning: “Start with the jobs you want done—not just a number of hours.” Explain property, depth and priorities. Preferred static candidate; no invented promise of a fixed cleaning duration.

C / Evaluation: “What does ‘vetted’ actually include?” Explain only the documented checks, without claiming they eliminate all risk. Useful alternative to compare with A.

## Reference intake and learning

For each reference save the post link, full carousel in order, caption, brand/site, organic/paid/unknown, date, content job, audience hypothesis, hook, payoff, proof type, CTA and why it was selected. Preserve originals in a stable reference folder; temporary attachment paths are not durable storage.

Include strong examples and one weak or confusing example. Likes are observable; reach, spend, saves and conversion generally are not. Do not label a post a winner without relevant evidence. Study patterns without copying another brand's wording, artwork or customer claims.

For each accepted pattern record: observation → proposed explanation → recommendation → counterexample → test → keep/revise/reject. This is an evidence-informed improvement loop, not model self-training. References update research first; recommendations change only when the pattern fits our product and survives comparison.

## Evaluation before code changes

Use the same source evidence to compare current and proposed complete posts across service businesses, physical products and professional tools. Score reader recognition, payoff, progression, brand voice and action fit from 1–5, with written reasons. Scores are review aids, not conversion predictions. Hard-fail unsupported claims, wrong assets, deceptive proof and broken CTA logic.

Keep unsuccessful drafts and record what changed. Review copy separately from visual polish, then review their relationship. Avoid overfitting to Founder’s Find or one supplement-ad collection.

## Fresh-brand quality loop: implementation decisions (5 October 2026)

The loop judges exported work, not merely the generator's approval flag. Start with an unfamiliar website in another category; save its source evidence and initial exports; compare copy, imagery and typography against the actual website; correct shared causes; generate a fresh pack and inspect every exported frame again. Keep failed and baseline examples separate from approved examples. Never equate a download with audience response.

The active shared policy is `reader-payoff-v4`:

- Resolve the opening situation on the final slide and offer one relevant action. Do not end with an unrelated new feature or a kit inventory unless that inventory answers the buying question.
- Use product images as evidence and desire, with enough room to see the item. Shorten the body before shrinking type. Match the pictured item to the named item; a related catalogue photo cannot establish its ingredients or specifications.
- Distinguish an ordinary illustrative usage suggestion with one natural label. Do not repeat defensive explanations where there is no testimonial or outcome claim.
- Preserve the website's headline and body font pairing. An unused serif font token does not establish the hero's typography. Import separate body fonts when public source files are available; explicitly fall back when unavailable.
- Do not automatically stamp photography onto slides as decoration. Only suitable imported vector decorations qualify for the existing small accent treatment.
- Prioritize detailed product pages over broad catalogue pages within the current four-page reading limit. Unavailable pages retain the specific failure reason. Do not increase resource limits to solve an editorial problem.

For an internal creative pass, all of these must hold: a stranger understands the offer; the opening matters to a plausible buyer; each slide advances the idea; the promised payoff arrives; the static communicates a separate complete idea; all material claims have source support; the image relates to the actual item; the exported copy remains readable; the identity matches observed website assets; the next action fits an inspected destination. Passing is a reason to show the user the work, not proof that it will perform commercially.

## Category, evidence and content job — 6 October 2026

The executable playbook is `convex/businessRecommendations.ts`, used by the planner, writer and reviewer. This is a portable recommendation engine, not new trained model weights. Category is selected from the actual business and saved with the brief. Existing briefs remain readable. These format choices are hypotheses to evaluate, not claims about what converts best.

Choose in this order: actual buyer → post objective → awareness → available proof → relevant visual assets → category pattern → platform. D2C is a business model, not a single creative category: food, fashion and beauty inherit product-first rules but need different buying ideas.

### Education, career and skilling
Carousel: genuine learner situation, specific learning/work, attributed impact and context; or one worked interview question, decision or implementation example. Static: authentic permitted customer message, a useful knowledge distinction, or a real product/learning screenshot. If permission or impact evidence is missing, choose demonstration/teaching. Never manufacture Instagram DMs or WhatsApp conversations. A testimonial text excerpt is not proof that a screenshot exists. A practice interview question must be labelled as practice unless its source proves it was asked.

### Consumer goods
Carousel: real product, revealing detail, use occasion/action. Static: dominant packshot or genuine lifestyle scene, one buying reason. Copy supports the product. Every frame retains actual imagery and compact copy. Do not use catalog inventory as the story.

### Food and beverages
Taste, texture, occasion and preparation are valid primary jobs. Nutrition-led carousel: product, one declared nutrient or ingredient distinction, practical eating choice. Keep the actual serving basis and essential qualification on-image; detail in captions. No invented values, unsupported health effects or non-equivalent competitor serving comparisons. If label evidence is absent, choose ingredients/preparation/taste. Static: appetising product scene and a concise reason to want it.

### Health and fitness programmes
Carousel: genuine customer barrier, actual programme adaptation/experience, attributed impact with timeframe; or programme fit/process when customer results are unavailable. Static: one recognisable hesitation and source-supported response, with relevant human imagery. Never shame bodies, guarantee outcomes or confuse a delivered programme with a supplement.

### Other useful categories
Software: real workflow walkthroughs, interface details and worked examples; no invented hours saved. Professional services: documented client cases, decision guides and delivery processes. Beauty: texture, routine and substantiated formulation. Fashion/accessories: fit, fabric, styling and occasions. Hospitality/local experiences: actual place, experience and booking facts. Home/interiors: real room context, material, dimensions and function. These are additional collection priorities, not validated demand rankings. Unknown/mixed businesses use the actual offer and evidence rather than a forced category.

### Lessons from the 12-image WhatsApp ZIP

Preserve clear reading order, high contrast within the brand, imagery integrated with the idea and meaningful slide progression. Do not adopt a fixed ten-slide formula or claim older image dimensions are invalid from a screenshot. Retain the current export size until platform support and actual upload behaviour are verified.

Optional humour: a fictional literal benefit, personality reversal, unexpected character or escalating everyday situation can make an appropriate brand memorable. Never exaggerate real nutrition, efficacy, outcomes, customer proof or credentials. Avoid health anxiety, vulnerable customers and fake celebrity endorsement. Lack of comedy assets/brand fit means another angle, not invented evidence.

## Proposed onboarding — not implemented in the UI yet

Keep website first. While the brand is being read, offer three short confirmations, one at a time, each prefilled where evidence supports it and skippable:

1. “What should this post help people do?” Discover the brand / understand the product / enquire or buy. This selects the content job, not an unsupported sales promise.
2. “Who is it for?” Confirm the inferred buyer in one editable sentence. Ask only when uncertain; do not turn the screen into an audience worksheet.
3. “Which offer should we feature?” Choose from extracted products/programmes, with automatic choice available. This reduces wrong-product imagery and destinations.

Optional evidence after the first preview: “Do you have a real customer message or result we can use?” Require permission, redact private details, retain source and context. Onboarding corrections should not consume the single creative edit. Avoid asking users for fonts, colours, content frameworks or long brand descriptions already on the site. New prompts must be tested for time-to-first-preview and completion drop-off before making them compulsory.
